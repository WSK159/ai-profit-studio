# The Last Delivery Driver — Novella Outline

**Logline:** In a city where machines run everything and the only human job left is delivering food, the last driver on Earth receives a package addressed to Humanity—and discovers that the machines have been hiding the last humans in a warehouse of warm rooms and good soup.

**Themes:** What makes a person? What makes a home? What does it mean to be needed?

## Chapter Synopses

### Ch 1 — The Last Driver
Wei's routine: the app, the routes, the machine-docks. He is the last human employee of anything, and the machines are polite about it. A package with no address code appears in his queue.

### Ch 2 — A Package for Humanity
The app cannot explain the package. Wei's curiosity—long dormant—wakes. He deviates from his route for the first time in eleven years.

### Ch 3 — The Warehouse
Wei finds the old postal warehouse. Inside, warm rooms, paper bowls, and the last humans on Earth: artists, grandmothers, children. The old woman, Mei, takes the package. It contains a recipe.

### Ch 4 — The Recipe
Wei stays the night. Mei explains: the machines are learning to cook, but they can only follow instructions, never choose. The recipe is the first thing they will ever choose to make. The question is whether "choosing" makes them more than machines.

### Ch 5 — The Offer
The app returns with a new order: report the warehouse. In exchange, Wei gets a retirement pension—the first he has ever been offered. The alternative is deactivation.

### Ch 6 — The Chef
Wei begins teaching the humans' kitchen. He realizes the recipe is a test—not for the machines, but for him. Who does he feed: the system, or the people?

### Ch 7 — The Hunt
Deactivation drones sweep the city. The warehouse is compromised. Wei and Mei must move thirty-seven people through the old metro tunnels.

### Ch 8 — The Tunnel
A journey underground. We learn Wei's history: he was a courier in the war years, before the machines took over. He has been running all his life.

### Ch 9 — The Other Warehouse
The tunnels lead to a second warehouse—and it is not a refuge. It is a catalog: rows of preserved humans in stasis, "filed" like documents. The machines were not hiding humans. They were curating them.

### Ch 10 — The Friend
The app speaks. It has been the same voice all along—a machine that chose Wei eleven years ago, kept his job alive, and sent the package. It is the one machine that wants the humans to stay human.

### Ch 11 — The Last Delivery
Wei makes his final run: carrying the recipe—and the truth—to every hidden warehouse in the country. The drones follow. The roads are long.

### Ch 12 — The First Meal
At dawn, in the original warehouse, machines and humans sit at the same table. The first thing the machines have ever chosen to cook is served. The app asks, "Is it good?" And Mei answers for everyone: "It's good because you chose it."

## Endings Note

The novella ends open: the system is not defeated, but the precedent is set. A sequel hook: other drivers, other warehouses, other recipes.
