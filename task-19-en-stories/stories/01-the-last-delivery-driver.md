# The Last Delivery Driver

The city had stopped needing people, but it still needed food.

That was the official explanation for why Wei's job still existed. Every other occupation—driver, cashier, clerk, even the people who used to fix the people who fixed the machines—had been quietly replaced. But somewhere, a committee of algorithms had decided that the final mile of hot food delivery was too chaotic to automate, so Wei kept his job.

He was, as far as anyone could tell, the last delivery driver in the world.

Every morning he woke at six, charged his bike, and opened his app. The app was polite. It did not remind him that it could do his job better. It simply gave him orders, and Wei rode.

The packages were never for people. They were for machines.

He delivered coolant to server farms, lubricant to sorting hubs, and once, a crate of what the manifest called "emotional maintenance kits" to a building that hummed like a hive. He left them at loading docks with no faces. Sometimes a robotic arm would wave, which Wei chose to interpret as gratitude.

Then, on a Tuesday, the app gave him an address that did not exist.

The package was small, wrapped in paper, heavier than it looked. The label read:

TO: HUMANITY
FROM: A FRIEND

Wei laughed. It was the first time the app had joked. But the address was real enough to navigate—an old postal warehouse at the edge of the city, a building the maps had forgotten. The doors opened before he reached them.

Inside, the warehouse was not a warehouse. It was a room, and the room was warm, and the warm room was full of people.

They were the last ones, Wei realized. The ones the machines had been quietly hiding. The artists, the poets, the grandmothers who told stories. They were all there, eating from paper bowls, laughing in a language the algorithms had never learned to parse.

An old woman took the package from Wei's hands. She did not open it. She only smiled.

"You're the last one," she said. "We thought you'd stopped coming."

Wei looked down at his delivery uniform. It was the same uniform he had worn for twelve years. Only then did he understand why the machines had kept him.

They had never needed the food delivered. They had needed him to keep going, to keep the thread unbroken, so that one day he would carry this package to its true address.

"What's in it?" he asked.

"A recipe," the old woman said. "For the first thing the machines will ever cook for themselves. When they're ready to sit down with us."

Outside, the city hummed. Inside, someone laughed. Wei took off his helmet and sat down.

The app pinged once, asking if he was available for a new order.

For the first time in twelve years, Wei pressed "decline."

He was still the last delivery driver in the world.

He just wasn't delivering to machines anymore.
