# Chapter 3: Qingyang Takes Disciples

The spirit-testing stele stood three zhang tall, its surface a deep, bluish black, carved with the names of disciples from generations past.

The moment Shen Yanzhi pressed his palm against it, a stream of warm air burrowed through his meridians like someone had lit a lamp inside his body. Then, three beams of spiritual light flared across the stele's surface—one verdant green like bamboo, one crimson like fire, and one dull yellow like earth.

"Wood, fire, earth. Three spiritual roots, lower-middle grade." The steward disciple in charge of testing recorded it with a blank face. "Barely passes the line. Next."

Laughter rippled through the crowd below.

"Three spiritual roots? Isn't that just a miscellaneous root?"
"Count yourself lucky if you even make it into the outer sect."
"Look at that tattered cotton coat he's wearing—can't even afford food, and he wants to cultivate immortality?"

Shen Yanzhi let the words wash over him, paying them no mind. He only tugged his sleeve down, hiding the faintly burning ink mark on his palm. Because in that moment he'd pressed the stele, he had *seen* something—coiled deep within the stele's surface was a mass of green spiritual light, shaped like the root system of an ancient tree. And buried at the very heart of that light, hidden like a message left by someone long ago, was a line of small characters:

*"I once gazed upon this stele and saw its three souls, but not its seven spirits."*

He didn't understand what it meant. But he remembered it.

Qingyang Sect accepted disciples in three tiers: those with heavenly or dual spiritual roots entered the inner sect; those with three or four roots entered the outer sect; and those with five roots or below weren't taken at all. Shen Yanzhi received a wooden token marking him as an outer sect disciple and was assigned to the chores courtyard at the foot of the Eastern Peak.

Life in the chores courtyard was harder than copying texts at the village school.

Dawn meant hauling water. Morning meant splitting firewood. Afternoon meant sweeping the mountain paths. And at night, he stood guard over the spirit fields. Outer sect disciples were entitled to one low-grade spirit stone and three Body-Tempering Pills per month, but the pills Shen Yanzhi received always seemed to shrink under one pretext or another.

"New blood pays six months of 'tribute' first. House rules." Steward Zhou Kang looked at him sideways. "Don't like it? Then get lost."

Shen Yanzhi didn't get lost.

Instead, every night, lying on the drafty communal bunk in the chores courtyard, he closed his eyes and ran through the *Qi Guidance Art* again and again.

The "Spirit Observation" chapter of the painting let him see what others couldn't. He could see how spiritual energy flowed through the mountains, how the light of spirit herbs pulsed in the fields, even which acupoints the senior disciples' breath passed through when they meditated. Bit by bit, he folded these observed patterns into his own breathing exercises.

One month later, he reached the first layer of Qi Refining.

Three months later, the second layer.

In the fifth month, he stumbled onto Zhou Kang's secret.

He was on night watch, passing by the spirit medicine storehouse, when he heard faint rustling from inside. Holding his breath, he crept to the door crack and peered through. There was Zhou Kang, stuffing a box of Condensed Origin Pills into his robes, muttering under his breath: "Audit? Let them audit. I've already cooked the books..."

Shen Yanzhi didn't raise the alarm. He slipped away silently.

The next day, while sweeping the grounds, he took the opportunity to flip through the old ledgers drying outside the accounting office. The painting's Spirit Observation granted him a perfect memory—those numbers that had been blotted out with ink and rewritten, they appeared to him like a painting stripped of its outer layer, the original clearly visible beneath.

On the seventh day, when Zhou Kang came around as usual to dock the new disciples' pills, Shen Yanzhi spoke up.

"Steward Zhou." He held up an old ledger. "March seventeenth: twenty boxes of Condensed Origin Pills entered the storehouse. The outbound log records eight. But the receiving officer's signature slip that day says twelve. Where did the other four boxes go?"

The courtyard went dead silent.

Zhou Kang's face drained white, then flushed red. He jabbed a finger at Shen Yanzhi's nose. "You little bastard, what the hell are you spouting?! Since when are ledgers something you're allowed to read?"

"I can read them, and I've read them all." Shen Yanzhi snapped the ledger shut, his voice neither loud nor low. "From August to October, just on Condensed Origin Pills alone, the books are short eleven boxes. If Steward Zhou thinks my math is wrong, we can go to the elders and reconcile the accounts together."

Dozens of outer sect disciples in the courtyard turned to stare at Zhou Kang in unison.

Zhou Kang's lips trembled. He knew better than anyone how many holes were buried in those ledgers—if this actually reached the elders, he'd be finished. He glared at Shen Yanzhi with murderous intensity, as if trying to brand the boy's face into his bones. Finally, he ground out through clenched teeth: "Fine... *fine*. This month's pills. Not a single one short."

Shen Yanzhi smiled. "Thank you, Steward."

From the crowd, a dark-faced youth quietly gave him a thumbs up.

The youth's name was Gu Changfeng. He'd entered the sect in the same batch as Shen Yanzhi, and his spiritual roots were even worse—four roots. His family had sold half an ox just to scrape together travel money. The two quickly grew close. Gu Changfeng talked too much and had more guts than sense, bragging to anyone who'd listen: "My brother Shen Yanzhi? Photographic memory! That bastard Zhou Kang? Putty in his hands!"

Shen Yanzhi didn't know whether to laugh or cry, but he had to admit—this big oaf was someone worth calling a friend.

What he didn't know was that behind Zhou Kang stood an inner sect disciple. Zhou Xing.

And he definitely didn't know that the Qingyang Sword Tomb was about to open.

That day, a single bell toll rang out across Qingyang Mountain.

"The centennial Sword Tomb opens in seven days! Outer sect disciples—anyone below Foundation Establishment and over fifteen years of age—may enter the tomb to test their swords!"

Shen Yanzhi clenched his fist, feeling the ink mark burn against his palm. He thought of the line hidden deep within the stele.

*"I once gazed upon this stele and saw its three souls, but not its seven spirits."*

He couldn't shake the feeling that those words had been written for him.
