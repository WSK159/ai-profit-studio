# Chapter 2: Night Visit to the Abandoned Temple

The news of the cultivators' arrival spread through Qingyang Town like wildfire.

The visitor was a patrol disciple from the Qingyang Sect, who had landed at the town entrance atop a cyan-patterned flying sword. Upon learning that the five-year recruitment period had come around, he left behind a "Spirit-Testing Notice" and announced that in three days, a platform would be set up at Qingyang Temple east of town to test spiritual roots. Any youth under fifteen was welcome to try.

The entire town went mad.

Shen Yanzhi, however, was distracted. He sat in the private school, brush in hand, copying texts, but his eyes kept drifting to his left palm. The ink-like mountain-and-river mark had faded somewhat since yesterday—if one didn't look closely, it might pass for a smudge of unwashed ink. But he knew better. The figure's back in the painting last night, those words—"All eight before you are dead"—he remembered them with perfect clarity.

After class let out, he slipped away alone, heading beyond the town's edge.

Three li outside Qingyang Town stood a derelict temple. What god it had once enshrined, no one could recall. The incense altar had long since toppled, and the roof let in shafts of sky. He'd only meant to take a shortcut home, but as he reached the temple gate, his palm suddenly burned with heat.

Driven by an impulse he couldn't name, he stepped inside.

The temple was silent, save for the wind moaning through broken windows. Shen Yanzhi circled the main hall once, found nothing, and was about to leave when the ground suddenly gave way beneath his foot—a loose blue brick flipped up, revealing a pitch-black cellar entrance beneath.

He hesitated for a moment, then descended the earthen steps.

The cellar was small, thick with the stale smell of old soil. In the corner sat a withered skeleton, its robes rotted to nothing, only the bones still maintaining an upright seated posture. Clutched in its arms was a stone casket. Carved across the casket's surface was a line of text, the strokes forceful and deep, as if etched with a sword:

*"In the eighteenth year of Tianyuan, the Star-Plucking Tower slaughtered my entire family. I survived nineteen years in hiding, only to send this away. To whoever comes after—if you obtain this object, remember: do not ascend the Star-Plucking Tower. Do not inquire into the matter of immortality."*

Shen Yanzhi's breath caught.

"Star-Plucking Tower." The Daoist last night hadn't mentioned those words. But staring at this inscription now, he suddenly understood—the dying man's ramblings hadn't been madness. *It killed my whole family.* It had slaughtered three hundred members of his sect. This stone casket might very well be the origin of that painting.

He bowed solemnly three times to the skeleton before opening the casket.

Inside lay only two items: a half-piece of green jade token, the front carved with "Qingyang" and the back with "Order," its broken edge smooth as if cleaved clean through by force; and a rolled bamboo slip. Unfurled, it revealed a simple breathing meditation manual called the *Guide Qi Art*—the wording plain, yet carrying an ancient, unadorned quality throughout.

Shen Yanzhi read the bamboo slip from beginning to end three times, memorizing it until the words rolled off his tongue without thought. Only then did he tuck the token against his skin, rebury the skeleton, replace the brick, and retreat from the temple.

He didn't return to town. Instead, he slipped into the old pine forest beyond the settlement.

Deep in the woods lay a large blue stone—a spot he'd often come to when herding cattle as a child. He sat cross-legged, following the methods of the *Guide Qi Art*, closed his eyes, focused his awareness on his dantian, and tried to sense the qi around him.

At first, he felt nothing.

He didn't grow impatient. He adjusted his breathing again and again. Who knew how long passed—perhaps half an hour, perhaps a full one—when suddenly, deep between his brows, something gave a faint pulse. The ink mark on his palm warmed in response, and the world before his eyes seemed to have a veil peeled away.

In the air, faint motes of light drifted.

Some glowed emerald, rising from grass and trees; some were pale gold, sinking into the soil; others floated in from the direction of town, carrying the breath of human habitation. They moved like living things, flowing past him in a slow current—but he couldn't grasp them.

The *Guide Qi Art* said the first step of Qi Refining was "sensing qi." Shen Yanzhi knew nothing of talent or aptitude. He only knew that he could truly see those lights now—and it wasn't an illusion. He looked down and saw a wisp of pale cyan qi thread coiling around his own fingertip.

He drew a deep breath and tried to guide that thread of qi into his dantian.

The instant the qi entered his body, the ink mark on his palm blazed bright. A small phantom of mountains and rivers flashed before his eyes, gone in a breath. Then, in his ears, came a soft sound—like the turning of a book's first page.

An archaic voice spoke at the bottom of his heart:

*"Picture Archive, First Entry: Spirit Observation—unlocked."*

*"Host's spiritual perception aptitude: top of the top."*

Shen Yanzhi's eyes snapped open.

Night had fallen. Crickets chirped all around the pine grove. Around the blue stone where he sat, those scattered motes of spiritual energy were slowly converging toward him, like streams returning to the sea.

He looked down at the ink mark on his palm, and suddenly, he smiled.

"Star-Plucking Tower… the matter of immortality." His voice carried the sharp, fearless edge of youth—the boldness of a newborn calf that knows no fear of tigers. "I'll try it anyway."

Three days later. Qingyang Temple.

Shen Yanzhi pushed through the sea of people, stepped up to the spirit-testing platform, and pressed his hand against the cold stone stele.
