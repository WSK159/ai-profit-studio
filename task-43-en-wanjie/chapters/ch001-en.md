# Chapter 1: The Fragment Map

Heaven's Origin Calendar, year 1072. Winter.

Qingyang Town was hit by its heaviest snowfall of the season—so heavy that even the beam of the private schoolmaster's draft-riddled hall bent under the weight. Shen Yanzhi added the last lump of coal to the brazier, rubbing his chapped, cracked hands together, when he heard a dull thud from outside—someone had collapsed in the snow.

He threw on his patched-over-patched cotton coat and pushed open the door. Snowflakes stung his face, nearly blinding him. Beneath the old locust tree at the mouth of the alley, a dark figure was curled up—a Daoist priest in tattered robes, ice crusting his beard and eyebrows, clutching an oilcloth bundle to his chest as if his life depended on it.

"Young... young man," the priest said, lips blue, voice barely a thread. "Please... could you spare a bowl of hot water?"

Shen Yanzhi didn't hesitate. He half-carried, half-dragged the man into the woodshed, built a fire, and warmed half a bowl of rice porridge over the stove. The priest gulped it down, recovering half a life's worth of strength, but his murky eyes never left the oilcloth bundle in his arms—as if it were his very soul.

"You're the schoolmaster's apprentice?" the priest asked suddenly.

"Yes." Shen Yanzhi nodded. "The master took me in. I copy books and sweep floors to pay for my keep."

"Good. Knowing characters is good, very good." The priest let out a low laugh, sounding like a broken bellows. He unwrapped the oilcloth, revealing a roll of yellowed silk painting. Mist and clouds swirled across its surface, mountains and rivers stretched in every direction, with faint glimpses of immortal palaces, cascading waterfalls, and flowing springs. It was only a foot square, yet it seemed to contain an entire world.

"Take it." The priest shoved the painting into his hands.

Shen Yanzhi froze. "Master Daoist, this is far too precious—"

"Precious?" The priest laughed again, and the laughter turned into a coughing fit that spat blood. "It killed my entire family. It killed my master. It killed all three hundred disciples of my sect. Tell me—is that precious enough for you?"

Shen Yanzhi's grip on the silk tightened. The priest stared at him, and in his murky eyes, a sliver of clarity suddenly blazed through. "Remember this: speak of this painting to no one. Heaven knows, earth knows, you know, I know. If anyone asks... say you never saw me."

"Then... what about you, Master Daoist?"

"Me?" The priest gazed out at the snow, his voice so light it seemed ready to float away. "The road I was meant to walk ends tonight."

Having said that, he fell silent. He leaned against the wall, staring into the flames of the brazier, and slowly closed his eyes.

Shen Yanzhi kept watch until dawn. When daylight came, the priest had stopped breathing—but strangely, his body hadn't stiffened. Instead, like a fading painting, it was turning transparent inch by inch. Shen Yanzhi stumbled back in shock, watching helplessly as the body dissolved into specks of ink, scattering into the air without leaving a single trace.

All that remained in the woodshed was the silk painting. And him.

He should have been terrified. But instead, he crouched down, picked up the painting, carefully rolled it back up, tucked it into his chest, and kowtowed three times toward where the priest had vanished. "Master Daoist, I don't care whether it's precious or cursed. You entrusted it to me, so I'll guard it for you."

That night, Shen Yanzhi slept in the woodshed, the silk painting tucked beneath his pillow. He woke shivering in the middle of the night and reached for the pillow—only to touch something ice-cold. The painting had somehow unfurled across his chest, and the mountains and rivers on its surface were glowing, as if a lamp were hidden within the silk.

Before he could cry out, the world spun around him.

When he opened his eyes again, he stood on an endless plain. There was no sky above, no ground below—only boundless mountains and rivers flowing past him: immortal palaces, ancient cities, wild wastelands, vast seas, rivers of stars—layer upon layer, like flipping through a book with no end. In the distance, atop a towering peak that scraped the heavens, sat a blurry figure, back turned to him. A voice descended from the nine heavens:

"One who enters this painting shall know its name—The Painting of Ten Thousand Worlds, the Everlasting World-Painting."

"What is your name?"

Shen Yanzhi opened his mouth and realized, to his surprise, that he wasn't afraid anymore. He raised his head, looking at that distant silhouette, and spoke word by word:

"This junior is Shen Yanzhi."

"Shen Yanzhi..." The figure repeated his name, seeming to smile. "Very well. From this day forth, you are the ninth master of this painting."

"And the previous eight?"

"The previous eight..." The figure's voice faded, like ashes scattered in the wind. "All dead."

Shen Yanzhi's whole body jolted, and he snapped awake.

Daylight flooded the room. He lay in the woodshed, his pillow empty—the painting was gone. Panicked, he patted himself down frantically, then felt a strange itch in his left palm. He opened his hand to find a thin ink-black line coiled across his palm, shaped like mountains and rivers, like a tiny brand.

He stared at the ink mark. Slowly, from the depths of his heart, a strange and indescribable tremor stirred.

Hurried footsteps sounded from outside. The schoolmaster's voice came through the door: "Yanzhi! Yanzhi! Come out quickly—the immortal sects have sent people!"

Shen Yanzhi clenched his fist, hiding the ink mark in his sleeve, and pushed open the woodshed door.

After the snow, the sky was clear. Above Qingyang Town, a streak of sword-light was splitting through the clouds.

He suddenly felt that his life—this life of his—was about to change from this very day.
